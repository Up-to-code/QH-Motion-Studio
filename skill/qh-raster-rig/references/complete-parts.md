# Complete parts and crop safety

A missing silhouette is an artwork failure. Reconstruct the complete hidden tail, thigh, waist or sleeve with the image generator; do not stretch another limb, crop harder, lower a gate or claim that padding restores missing anatomy. Preserve accepted reference and original PNGs. Request one complete independent part per generation, genuine alpha, clear margins and solid overlap behind the joint. Inspect generated alpha and colors: reject glow, blurred halos, unrelated pieces and cropped ends. Fox tail, hips and trouser legs are regenerated examples in `animations/fox/source/regenerated-v2/`.

`sourceMargins` computes the alpha bounding box and all four clear margins at native image resolution. A declared `sourcePolicy.minMarginPx` makes empty images or insufficient source margins fail acceptance independently of viewport/canvas clipping. Review source-crop-risk findings as source images, not canvas crops. This test detects boundary risk; it does not prove semantic completeness inside the margins. Inspect the full source silhouette as well.

Preserve anatomy when adding margin m: asset size S becomes S+2m; image pivot p becomes p+m; material point q becomes q+m. T(-p-m)(q+m)=q-p, so the world point and every child joint stay unchanged. Add m to rig and per-motion contact points. Do not use resizePart to pad an image: scaling child offsets changes anatomy. `padPartCoordinates` implements the coordinate shift. Replacement expressions must receive the same margin and registration as their base part; motion presets must resolve registered replacement assets from the rig.

A mesh additionally needs a domain {size, pivot, offset}. Its size/pivot describe the original deformation domain; padding increases only offset. Evaluate weight/interpolation at coordinate minus offset. This preserves surface displacement and tangent, including clothing attachments. Intentional resize scales all domain coordinates together. Tests compare deformed material points, child joints and contacts before/after padding.

Joining complete parts also requires draw-order review: the hip connector belongs behind the thigh layers. A connector drawn over the thighs looks like shorts even with perfect joint math. Alpha overlap gates cannot certify garment silhouette or matching shading; inspect rest pose, extrema, replacement transitions and delivery size.
